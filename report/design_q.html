Amendment Q: the queue starts at the stop line (registered 9 November 2026, after the v6 grid)

This is an amendment to DESIGN_H.md, written after the 960-episode v6 grid (runs/grid/R01) had finished and been analysed. The registration integrity/REGISTRATION_FREEZE_H.json and every v6 file stay as they are. Amendment Q has its own code copy (amend_Q/), gates, freeze and runs.

1. Why: in the v6 grid the LPI never bound

In all 480 concurrent/LPI pairs of the v6 grid, the measures of the LPI run are identical to its concurrent twin. Only the run name differs. H6a–c and H7 are therefore exactly 0 with zero-width intervals. Nothing failed in the LPI code:

  • the signal state, the SUMO program and the pre-entry red mask all switch at 5 s;
  • the lead car's trajectory is identical in both programs.

The cause is the start of the queue.

  • Distance. The lead car spawns with its front 8.2 m behind the junction entry (route s = 26.8 m against 35.0 m).
  • Launch lag. It starts from rest under the copied stock-pedal tracker, whose throttle rises at only 0.08 per second below 0.15 m/s. The car therefore crawls for about 4 s before it accelerates.
  • Effect. The lead car first enters the junction 8.95 s after the green begins. The pre-entry red cap (√(0.6·distance)) stays above its speed throughout the 5 s LPI, so the cap never binds.
  • Result. In both programs the walkers already had about 9 s on the zebra before the turn could reach them. The "concurrent" program behaved as a de facto leading interval.

Why the gate missed it. Gate G6d checked only that a single LPI run completed and that no car entered the junction before 5 s. That check passed trivially, at 8.95 s. It had no concurrent twin to compare against. This is a gate-design error, and it is reported as such.

What still stands from the v6 grid.

  • The lead-paradigm contrasts (H5) and the follower-law estimates (H8).
  • The exploratory breakdowns.

What the v6 grid shows about the LPI. A 5 s LPI changes nothing when the turning queue starts this far upstream with this launch. That is reported as a finding of the benchmark, not as a test of H6/H7.

2. The one change (marked v6q)

In amend_Q/runner_v6q.py (a copy of native/runner_v6.py), --queue-offset-m d moves all three cars d metres forward along their route. d = 0 reproduces runner_v6 exactly (gate Q-a).

  • Amendment Q uses d = 7.4 m. The lead car's front is then 0.8 m behind the junction entry when the green begins, and the followers keep their 10 m spacing.
  • Why this is the relevant case. A leading pedestrian interval exists for a turning queue that stands at the stop line when the green starts.
  • Unchanged: the scenarios, planners, follower laws, signal programs, LPI duration (5 s), pass rule, measures, horizon, servers and seeds.

3. Gates (criteria fixed before the gate runs; runs/gates/Q)

  • Q-a (reproduction). With d = 0, two runs give the same MEASURES.json and PROGRESS.json as their v6 grid counterparts, apart from the run name:
    • S1_B5_01__P1__idm__lpi (scripted planner);
    • S1_B1_01__P2__idm__concurrent (rule service).
  • Q-b (the LPI binds). With d = 7.4, run S0_201, S1_B5_01 and S1_B1_01 under P1 with IDM, in both programs. For each pair:
    • the lead car's first junction entry (first_junction_entry_t) is ≥ 5.0 s under LPI;
    • it is at least 3.0 s later under LPI than under concurrent (the launch takes about 4 s, so under concurrent the entry falls near 5 s rather than before it);
    • both runs complete;
    • no run ends in a mutual standstill.
  • Q-c (sanity). With d = 7.4, S1_B5_01__P3__gipps__lpi completes without a standstill.
  • Contacts. Polygon contacts are counted and reported as in v6. A contact does not fail a gate unless it begins under the pass rule (the v6 G6b criterion).
  • If a gate fails. Change only the offset, record the change here, and rerun the gate.

Gate outcome (9 November, 22:13; checkpoints/gates/GATES_Q.json). All passed:

  • Q-a 2 of 2: identical to v6;
  • Q-b 6 of 6: lead junction entry 5.1 s concurrent against 10.1 s LPI in each pair, with the encounters now differing between the twins;
  • Q-c 1 of 1.
  • There were no contacts and no standstills. Nothing was changed after the gates.

4. Grid and analysis

  • Grid: the same 960 episodes as BATCH_GRID_R01.json (80 scenarios × 3 lead paradigms × 2 follower laws × 2 signal programs), each with queue_offset_m = 7.4, in runs/grid/Q01. Each runs once, with no outcome-selected retries.
  • Registered analysis. analysis/aggregate_v6.py and analysis/select_replay_v6.py are unchanged and write to checkpoints/Q01. The hypotheses are H5–H8 and Check C, exactly as in DESIGN_H.md section 5, with the same directions, contrasts, bootstrap (4000, fixed seed) and replay rule.
  • Exploratory analysis. posthoc/micro_macro_v6.py is unchanged and writes to checkpoints/Q01/posthoc.

5. How the two grids are reported

  • Primary for H6 and H7: Q01. It is the only grid in which the LPI can act.
  • H5 and H8. Both grids are reported. R01 is the registered original; Q01 shows whether the paradigm and follower effects hold when the queue starts at the line.
  • The upstream start. The R01 "LPI changes nothing" result is reported as a benchmark finding: when an LPI matters depends on where the queue is when the green begins.
  • Labelling. Every Q01 number is labelled as coming from the amendment. The amendment was written after the R01 outcomes were known; it changes only the start of the queue, and the hypotheses are unchanged.
