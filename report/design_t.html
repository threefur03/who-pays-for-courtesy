Amendment T: state-triggered LLM scenarios (designed 10 November 2026, after the Q01 results)

This amendment tests the generator diagnostic in the paper: scenarios written in open loop, with clock-timed arrivals, land where the closed-loop dynamics put them, often away from the planner under test.

It adds one new generator arm with state triggers, and one control arm that isolates a confound found while designing.

  • Registration: integrity/REGISTRATION_FREEZE_T.json, written only after every gate below passes.
  • Isolation: the registered v6 files, freeze H and freeze Q stay as they are. Amendment T has its own code (amend_T/), scenarios (amend_T/scenarios/), gates, runs (runs/gates/T, runs/grid/T01) and checkpoints (checkpoints/T01).

1. Why, and the confound found while designing

What Q01 showed. In the stop-line grid (Q01), the 40 LLM scenarios (S1) were weaker tests of the lead planner than the 40 calibrated ones:

  • the lead car met no crosser in any of its 12 runs in 9 of 40 scenarios (calibrated: 2);
  • the planners' cost to people spanned more than 1 person·s in 25 of 40 (calibrated: 36);
  • followers made 51% of the close passes (calibrated: 44%).

Two causes cannot be told apart from Q01 alone.

  • (a) Stale timing in the prompt. The v5 prompt told the LLM that "the lead car usually reaches the zebra about 23–26 s after the start". In Q01 the queue starts at the stop line, and the unimpeded lead car reaches the zebra at 14.3 s (19.3 s under the LPI), measured from the P1 runs.
    • The LLM wrote clock times for a timing the benchmark no longer had.
  • (b) Clock timing in closed loop. Even with correct timing, a clock-timed arrival cannot follow the closed-loop variation of the vehicles.
    • The lead car's zebra arrival ranges from 14.3 to 55.9 s across scenarios, and its median is 14.3 s under the reactive planner against 29.1 s under the courteous one.
    • The LPI adds 5 s.

2. Arms

All arms use:

  • the same model (anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5 via OpenRouter, temperature 0.8, max 3000 tokens);
  • the same eight briefs (scenario_generator_v5/generator/briefs.json);
  • the same admissible box, revision rule (two revisions with the checker's reasons) and duplicate rule (feature distance ≥ 0.05 within a brief);
  • five admitted scenarios per brief (40 per arm).

The LLM never sees a run outcome.

Arm Scenarios Prompt Runs
C0 the existing S1 set (frozen in v5) v5 prompt (lead reaches the zebra "23–26 s") reused from Q01: IDM, 3 planners × 2 signals = 240 runs
C1 new, clock-timed v5 prompt with one sentence corrected to the stop-line timing (§3) 40 × 3 planners × IDM × 2 signals = 240
T1 new, state-triggered the C1 prompt plus a trigger option (§3) 40 × 3 planners × IDM × 2 signals = 240

Common settings. Every run uses the queue at the stop line (offset 7.4 m, as in Q01), IDM followers, the stopped-car pass rule, the Q01 horizon and the Q01 servers.

3. The prompt changes, verbatim

C1 and T1. The v5 timing sentence is replaced by:

The lead car starts at the stop line, 20 m before the zebra, and usually reaches the zebra about 14 s after the start when it is not held up (about 19 s under a leading pedestrian interval, and later when it yields); the cars behind it follow about 7-20 s apart.

T1 only. One more paragraph:

Instead of release_s, any walker, the runner, an e-bike or the motorcycle may be released by a trigger tied to one of the three queued cars: "trigger": {"car": "lead"|"second"|"third", "to_zebra_m": d, "delay_s": x} releases it x seconds after that car's front comes within d metres of the zebra along its path (d 0-20, x 0-15). The lead car is the one under test. Use a trigger when the interaction should meet a particular car whatever the planner or the signal does; walkers still need about 5-11 s from release to reach the zebra.

Admissibility.

  • A triggered object has a trigger and no release_s.
  • car must be lead, second or third; to_zebra_m must be in 0–20 and delay_s in 0–15.
  • Every other bound is unchanged.
  • For the duplicate check only, a trigger is mapped to the clock time at which the unimpeded lead car would fire it: 4.5 + (20 − d) / 2.2 s, plus 7 s for the second car and 14 s for the third, plus the delay.

4. Runner change (amend_T/runner_v7t.py, marked v7t)

runner_v7t is a copy of amend_Q/runner_v6q.py.

  • A compiled scenario may carry "triggers": {object_id: {"car", "to_zebra_m", "delay_s"}}. Its triggered objects carry release_s = 1e6 until the trigger fires.
  • Each step: if the named car's front is within to_zebra_m of the zebra along its route (car_crossing_s[0] − front_s ≤ d), the object's release time becomes t + delay_s. From then on the unchanged clock release logic spawns it.
  • Logging: fire times go to TRIGGERS.json and into CONFIG.
  • Equivalence: without triggers, the runner is equivalent to runner_v6q (gate T-a).

5. Measures (per arm, over its 40 scenarios)

  1. miss. Scenarios in which the lead car meets no crosser (no encounter within the 5 s window) in any of its six runs.
  2. separation.
    • Scenarios in which the three planners' people held + slowed (each planner averaged over the two signals) spans more than 1 person·s.
    • Also the median span.
  3. lead share. Lead-car close passes (PET < 1.5 s) divided by all close passes, pooled over the arm's runs; the interval is a bootstrap over scenarios.
  4. brief realised. The v5 event detectors (event_B1–event_B7) under gap acceptance (P2), IDM, concurrent, for the 35 scenarios with a checkable brief (B1–B7).
  5. Descriptive. People held + slowed, vehicle standing, close passes per episode, by planner and signal.

6. Hypotheses (directional; T1 against C1)

Primary contrasts:

Prediction Test
H9 T1 has fewer miss scenarios than C1 one-sided Fisher exact test
H10 T1 has more separating scenarios than C1 one-sided Fisher exact test
H11 the lead share of close passes is higher in T1 than in C1 bootstrap of the difference over scenarios (4000 resamples, seed 21), one-sided 95%
  • H12, timing information: C1 against C0 on the same three measures, two-sided, reported as estimates.
  • Brief realisation: T1, C1 and C0 are reported against each other with no direction.
  • The three primary contrasts (H9–H11) are not corrected for multiplicity.
  • Interpretation:
    • H12 isolates cause (a).
    • H9–H11 isolate cause (b), given correct timing.
    • If T1 does not beat C1, the paper says that clock timing with correct information was enough, or that triggers did not help.

Gate record.

T-a passed (10 November, about 12:40). Both runs were identical to their Q01 twins.

T-b failed its third criterion, then passed on a corrected criterion.

  • The trigger mechanism worked in both runs.
    • ped_0 fired at 9.65 s, the step at which the lead car came within 10 m.
    • ped_run fired 2.00 s after the third car came within 5 m (44.2 → 46.2 s under P1, 64.5 → 66.5 s under P3).
    • CONFIG carried the realised release times.
  • The failed criterion: "ped_0's fire time differs between P1 and P3" failed, at 9.65 s in both runs.
    • The cause is the criterion, not the code. Both planners drive the same until the lead car is 30% through the turn, which lies beyond the 10 m point.
    • So a lead-car trigger at 10 m cannot follow the planner. A trigger on a car whose arrival depends on the planner does.
  • The correction. The criterion was corrected after this result to "the third car's trigger (ped_run) fires at different times under P1 and P3". That gave 44.2 against 64.5 s, so T-b passed.
  • What did not change: the code, the hypotheses, the measures and the arms.
  • Consequence for interpretation: a lead-car trigger adapts to the launch, the queue position and the LPI (all before the turn), but not to the lead planner's own yielding. A trigger on the second or third car also adapts to the planner.
