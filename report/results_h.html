1. The LPI never bound (all H6 and H7 contrasts are exactly 0)

What happened. All 960 episodes completed. In all 480 concurrent/LPI pairs, the LPI run's MEASURES.json and PROGRESS.json equal its concurrent twin's, apart from the run name. The lead car's trajectory is identical too.

Why. The cause is the start of the queue, not the LPI code:

  • the lead car's front starts 8.2 m behind the junction entry;
  • the copied stock-pedal tracker raises the Micra's throttle at only 0.08/s below 0.15 m/s, so the car crawls for about 4 s before accelerating;
  • it first enters the junction 8.95 s after the green begins;
  • the pre-entry red cap stays above its speed throughout the 5 s LPI.

So both programs gave walkers about 9 s before the turn could reach them.

Why the gate missed it. Gate G6d checked one LPI run and "no car enters before 5 s". That passed trivially, because no concurrent twin was compared.

What follows.

  • H6a, H6b, H6c and H7 are not identifiable from R01. They are not supported, and they are not refuted either.
  • Amendment Q (DESIGN_Q.md) reruns the grid with the queue at the stop line, which is where an LPI acts.
  • As a benchmark finding: with an upstream queue and this launch, a 5 s LPI changes nothing for anyone in any of the 480 pairs.
  • Measure artefact. M1 bins encounters by time since the car's green. Under LPI, that clock starts 5 s later, so identical encounters move one 5 s bin earlier. The R01 LPI curve in Figure 4a is therefore a relabelled concurrent curve and must not be read as an effect.

4. Exploratory, microscopic

M1, who goes first.

  • No simulated car encounter falls in the first 15 s of green: the queue enters the junction about 9 s after the green begins and must then cover the 19 m to the zebra.
  • In the 15–20 s bin the person went first in 0.22 of encounters; in 20–25 s, 0.66; later, 0.13–0.42.
  • Encounter-level person-first share by planner: reactive 0.35, gap acceptance 0.34, courteous 0.33. These are close, because courteous lead cars meet fewer people at all (see M2), not because they let more go first.
  • SinD (real, concurrent): 8 of 15 car-first in < 10 s, 21 of 68 later. The simulated queue arrives too late to reproduce SinD's early-green encounters. Amendment Q moves the queue to the line and addresses that.

M2, close passes (PET < 1.5 s) by vehicle role, pooled:

Role Encounters Close share Median PET
lead 1024 0.32 [0.24, 0.41] 2.6 s
followers 1658 0.46 [0.37, 0.55] 2.05 s
motorcycle 1794 0.21 [0.16, 0.26] 2.2 s
  • The followers produce the closest passes, even though the planner only controls the lead car.
  • Lead close share by planner: reactive 0.34 (456 encounters), gap acceptance 0.28 (312), courteous 0.32 (256). The courteous planner's benefit is fewer lead encounters, not gentler ones.
  • Per episode: lead 1.07 encounters (0.34 close), followers 1.73 (0.79 close), motorcycle 1.87 (0.40 close).

M3, held + slowed per person who entered:

Crosser Reactive Gap acceptance Courteous
walkers 1.34 s 1.00 s 0.88 s

  • The runner pays the most, and courtesy does not reduce its cost. The cause has not been analysed.
  • Walkers and e-bike riders gain from courtesy.
