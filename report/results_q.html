6. Post hoc additions (after reading sections 1–3; not in any registration)

The LPI's effect on people, by source. LPI − concurrent in person held + slowed, paired bootstrap over scenarios (4000 resamples, seed 11):

  • S0 (calibrated): −2.73 [−4.45, −1.11];
  • S1 (LLM): −0.33 [−2.76, 2.01];
  • pooled: −1.53 [−3.04, −0.10].

Together with H6c by source, the LPI lowered both people's cost and cars' standing in the calibrated scenarios. In the LLM scenarios it raised standing without detectably helping people.

Who is at the kerb when the walk signal starts. Walkers with release_s ≤ 3 s, from each scenario's frozen population:

  • calibrated: 3 of 5 walkers on average, in 40 of 40 scenarios;
  • LLM: 0.42 of 4.5, in 15 of 40 scenarios.

A leading interval can only give a head start to people who are there when it starts. This difference is consistent with the source split, but it is not a tested explanation.
